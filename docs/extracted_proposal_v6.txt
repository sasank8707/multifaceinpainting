Introduction — Why Plausible Completion Is Not Enough
Mask-Aware Multi-Reference Fusion  |  Identity -Preserving Facial Inpainting 2
THE CORE PROBLEM
•  Facial inpainting reconstructs missing or occluded facial regions in an image.
•  A perceptually realistic face can represent the wrong person — realism ≠ 
identity fidelity.
•  Standard benchmarks (SSIM, PSNR, FID) do not penalise identity drift.
•  Under severe occlusion (>50% mask), discriminative identity cues vanish from 
the input.
•  Biometric systems demand reconstructed faces pass face-verification — not 
just look good.
TASK TAXONOMY
Inpainting: Fills missing/masked regions using surrounding context.
Restoration: Removes degradation (blur, noise) from an existing complete image.
Editing: Semantically alters attributes (age, expression, hair) of a face.
Swapping: Replaces the full identity of one face with another — different person.
→ This work targets inpainting. The reconstructed region must match the subject's own 
identity.

Research Question & Research Gap
Mask-Aware Multi-Reference Fusion  |  Identity -Preserving Facial Inpainting 3
PRIMARY RESEARCH QUESTION
Can region-specific evidence from multiple identity references improve biometric fidelity under severe facial occlusion without 
sacrificing realism?
Secondary: Can natural-language guidance provide controllable attribute correction during reconstruction?
RESEARCH GAP — GLOBAL REFERENCE USE IS INSUFFICIENT
Core gap:  Existing methods use reference identity information globally, even though different references contain unequal evidence for the specific facial region being 
reconstructed.
Implication:  A reference showing a different pose may perfectly capture the ear but nothing about the nose — applying it globally dilutes thesignal.
Missing:  No existing method scores references conditioned on mask geometry before fusing identity tokens.
Testable:  Identity fidelity should improve as reference relevance to the masked region increases — directly measurable.

Research Objectives
Mask-Aware Multi-Reference Fusion  |  Identity -Preserving Facial Inpainting 4
O1 — RETRIEVE
Score references using pose, quality, visibility, and 
overlap with the missing facial region.
▸ Estimate facial pose (yaw, pitch) for each reference.
▸ Compute face -quality score (sharpness, 
illumination, occlusion rate).
▸ Measure landmark visibility within the masked 
region.
▸ Score geometric overlap of each reference with the 
mask.
▸ Rank references; select top -K per region-of-interest.
O2 — FUSE
Construct spatial identity tokens from the most 
informative reference regions.
▸ Extract patch -level identity features aligned to 
landmarks.
▸ Apply mask-conditioned cross -attention to weight 
regional tokens.
▸ Aggregate multi -reference tokens with a learned 
weighted sum.
▸ Inject fused identity tokens into the diffusion 
conditioning path.
O3 — VERIFY
Rerank or regenerate candidates using identity, 
realism, and structural constraints.
▸ Generate K candidates from the diffusion model.
▸ Score each on identity similarity (held -out face 
matcher).
▸ Score on realism (NIQE / BRISQUE) and structural 
fidelity.
▸ Select candidate maximising the joint quality -
identity score.
▸ Discard unrealistic images even if matcher similarity 
is high.
Note: Text-guided attribute arbitration and inference efficiency are deferred to future work / optional experiments.

VERIFIER-AWARE SAMPLING - Mechanism (Inference-Time Reranking, NOT a Training Loss)
At inference: diffusion samples K independent outputs (K=8 -16, stochastic). A held -out ArcFace -R100 matcher + NIQE quality estim ator score each candidate. Final output = argmax( 
lambda*ID_similarity + (1 -lambda)*quality ). The verifier never receives gradients - it is a test-time reranker only.
KEY DESIGN DECISIONS
* Mask-conditioned scoring (O1): References scored by geometric overlap with mask - not global quality. T op-K selected per region.
* Regional token fusion (O2): Patch-level identity features weighted by mask-region relevance via cross-attention; injected into UNet.
* Verifier-aware sampling (O3) = test-time reranker: K candidates sampled; held-out verifier + quality score select best. No gradient through verifier.

Evaluation Plan - Identity Fidelity vs. Mask Severity
Mask-Aware Multi-Reference Fusion  |  Identity -Preserving Facial Inpainting 13
PRIMARY EXPERIMENT
Identity Fidelity vs. Mask Severity
Four severity levels:  25%  |  50%  |  75%  |  Full-face
Full-face: when 100% is masked, identity evidence comes entirely from the reference set, 
directly testing retrieval and fusion in isolation.
Metrics & Protocol
* Identity (ArcFace-R100): Cosine similarity + TAR@FAR=0.1% + AUC of ROC. Matcher is 
held out - not used in training or A7 selection.
* FID: Computed over >=5 000 reconstructed vs. ground-truth crops (not vs. training 
set). Measures distributional realism.
* NIQE / BRISQUE: No-reference per-image quality. Catches unrealistic outputs that 
may fool FID.
* Landmark error: Mean L2 distance (px) between predicted and GT landmarks after 
reconstruction - structural fidelity measure.
* CLIP score: Text-guided experiments only: cosine(CLIP(output), CLIP(prompt)). 
Evaluates attribute preservation (e.g. 'wearing glasses').
ABLA TION CONDITIONS
A1  Single reference (random)
A2  Random multi-reference, no scoring
A3  Globally best reference (quality, no mask-aware)
A4  Mean multi-reference fusion (equal weights)
A5  Global attention fusion (no mask conditioning)
A6  Proposed mask-aware regional fusion, no verifier
A7  Proposed fusion + verifier-aware sampling  [FULL]
Held-out matcher: ArcFace-R100 (MS1MV3) - NOT used in training loss or A7 selection.
Verification: reconstructed face vs. >=3 withheld references per identity. Report cosine sim + 
TAR@FAR=0.1% + AUC.
