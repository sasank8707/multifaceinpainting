# Real-World Validation Framework: Automatic Damage Mask Detection & Inpainting

This document defines the real-world validation framework, safety protocols, quality assessment, and empirical standing of the automatic damage detection pathway (**Path B: No-Mask Face Restoration**) in the AI Image Restoration System.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Scientific Integrity & Scope Notice**:
> The automatic damage detector (`DamageMaskDetector`) is an **engineering input convenience** designed to estimate damage regions when a user does not supply an explicit binary mask. It is **NOT** a peer-reviewed or scientifically validated clinical burn/scar segmentation model. We explicitly distinguish **engineering validation** (verifying software plumbing, invariance preservation, and crash-free execution) from **research validation** (evaluating multi-reference identity preservation under severe occlusion). Zero claims of natural burn, scar, or lesion detection efficacy are made until authentic clinical/natural cases are evaluated and manually verified.

## 4. Current Limitations

1. **Unsupervised Heuristic Modeling**: `DamageMaskDetector` uses CIELAB color outlier modeling and morphological texture residuals (Top-Hat / Black-Hat). Strong non-uniform shadows or heavy cosmetics (e.g. eyeliner, contouring) can produce false-positive anomaly saliency.
2. **Partial Occlusion Capture**: On synthetic occlusions with gradual boundaries, the detector tends to capture only the highest-contrast core, potentially leaving lower-contrast lesion fringes unmasked.
3. **Severe Landmark Destruction**: If a trauma completely destroys both eyes or obliterates the jawline, MediaPipe FaceMesh cannot fit landmarks. In this case, the `LANDMARKS_UNRELIABLE` or `NO_FACE_DETECTED` safety check will reject the image to prevent misaligned reconstruction.
4. **Multi-Subject Inpainting**: If multiple people are in the frame, the pipeline flags `MULTIPLE_FACES_DETECTED` and halts to prevent identity ambiguity.
